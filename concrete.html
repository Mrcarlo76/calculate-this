Calculate concrete for slabs, footings and rectangular pours

For a rectangular pour, concrete volume is simply the plan area multiplied by the concrete depth. Use metres for length and width; this calculator accepts the depth in millimetres and converts it automatically. The net volume is shown separately from the extra allowance so you can see exactly what is being added before ordering.

Formula: length (m) × width (m) × depth (m) = concrete volume (m³). For a 150 mm slab, use a depth of 0.15 m; the calculator performs that conversion for you.

For irregular areas, divide the pour into simple rectangular sections, calculate each net volume, add them together, then apply a suitable allowance to the combined total. Site levels, excavation tolerance, formwork and sub-base conditions can all affect the actual quantity required.

Using the result for ready-mix concrete

The calculator's Concrete to order figure is the net volume plus your chosen allowance. It is a planning quantity rather than a supplier instruction: ready-mix suppliers can have their own minimum-load, part-load and delivery rules, and the final order should always be checked against measured site conditions immediately before the pour.

Concrete calculator questions

How do I convert concrete depth from mm to metres?

Divide millimetres by 1,000. For example, 150 mm is 0.15 m and 300 mm is 0.30 m.

How much extra concrete should I allow?

The calculator starts at 5%, but the right allowance depends on the pour, ground and formwork tolerances, access and site conditions. Change it to match the job rather than treating 5% as a fixed rule.

Does this work as a ready-mix calculator?

Yes for rectangular pours: the result gives the total cubic metres after your chosen allowance, which can be used as a starting point when discussing a ready-mix order.

What about an irregular slab?

Split it into rectangles, calculate each section, add the net volumes together and then apply the allowance to the combined quantity.
